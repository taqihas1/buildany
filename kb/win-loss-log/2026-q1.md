# Win/Loss Log: Q1 2026

## Summary

Q1 was a strong quarter with a 58% win rate. We lost 4 opportunities, all to specific competitors with clear patterns. The "no decision" was a healthcare client that delayed their ERP project indefinitely due to budget constraints.

## Wins

### 1. Cloud Migration — Energy Sector ($620K)
**Client**: Midstream oil & gas company
**Competitor**: Accenture
**Why We Won**: Speed to value. We proposed a 16-week engagement vs. their 9-month timeline. Client had a hard deadline for divestiture.

**Key Factor**: Pre-built Azure landing zone accelerator reduced setup time from 6 weeks to 2 weeks.

### 2. Oracle ERP Implementation — Healthcare ($720K)
**Client**: Regional hospital network
**Competitor**: Deloitte
**Why We Won**: Oracle specialization. Deloitte proposed a "flexible" team with varying Oracle experience. We committed a dedicated Oracle Cloud team with 50+ certifications.

**Key Factor**: Client reference from similar-sized healthcare network who attended our pitch presentation.

### 3. Data Analytics Platform — Retail ($340K)
**Client**: National specialty retailer
**Competitor**: Slalom
**Why We Won**: Industry-specific use cases. We demonstrated pre-built retail analytics dashboards (inventory, customer cohorts, CLV) that Slalom would have built from scratch.

**Key Factor**: Proof-of-concept in 2 weeks using their sample data.

### 4. Cybersecurity Assessment — Financial Services ($180K)
**Client**: Regional bank
**Competitor**: Coalfire (security-only firm)
**Why We Won**: Business context. Coalfire delivered a technical vulnerability report. We delivered a business-risk-ranked roadmap with estimated breach cost modeling.

**Key Factor**: CISO appreciated the "board-ready" presentation format.

### 5. SAP S/4HANA Migration — Manufacturing ($890K)
**Client**: Industrial equipment manufacturer
**Competitor**: IBM
**Why We Won**: Fixed-price certainty. IBM proposed time & materials with a wide range. We offered fixed-price with defined scope boundaries, reducing executive anxiety.

**Key Factor**: Detailed discovery phase (2 weeks, no charge) that built trust before contract.

### 6. Salesforce Implementation — Healthcare ($250K)
**Client**: Medical device distributor
**Competitor**: Cognizant
**Why We Won**: Nearshore cost advantage. Similar quality at 35% lower cost due to our Mexico delivery center.

**Key Factor**: Real-time demo with bilingual (English/Spanish) support team.

### 7. Managed Services — Technology ($420K annual)
**Client**: SaaS startup (post-Series B)
**Competitor**: Rackspace
**Why We Won**: Scale-with-me model. Rackspace required 3-year commitment. We offered month-to-month with annual reviews, matching their growth uncertainty.

**Key Factor**: DevOps-as-a-service component that Rackspace didn't offer.

## Losses

### 1. Cloud Migration — Manufacturing ($1.2M) ❌
**Client**: Fortune 500 automotive OEM
**Winner**: McKinsey & Company
**Why We Lost**: Brand prestige. Client explicitly said "we need a name the board recognizes." Our technical proposal scored higher but lost on "organizational risk."

**Lesson**: For Fortune 500, lead with brand credentials and Big Four alumni on the team. Consider partnership strategy.

### 2. Oracle ERP — Public Sector ($450K) ❌
**Client**: State government agency
**Winner**: Local incumbent vendor
**Why We Lost**: Incumbent advantage. They had a 10-year maintenance relationship and "sole source" justification.

**Lesson**: Public sector requires 18-month relationship building. Don't compete on short-cycle RFPs without existing presence.

### 3. Data Platform — Financial Services ($680K) ❌
**Client**: Insurance company
**Winner**: Palantir
**Why We Lost**: AI hype. Palantir positioned their "ontology" approach as AI-first. Our pragmatic approach sounded "boring" to a CEO chasing AI headlines.

**Lesson**: For C-suite audiences, lead with AI/ML narrative even when the core work is integration. Reframe traditional data engineering as "AI infrastructure."

### 4. Cybersecurity Implementation — Healthcare ($320K) ❌
**Client**: Children's hospital
**Winner**: Smaller boutique (2-person firm)
**Why We Lost**: Price. The boutique bid 40% below us. Client chose to accept the risk of limited resources for the cost savings.

**Lesson**: For non-profit healthcare, have a "lean team" option ready. We only proposed our standard team template.

## Patterns Identified

1. **We win on speed and specialization**
2. **We lose to brand prestige at Fortune 500**
3. **We lose to incumbent relationships in public sector**
4. **We lose to AI hype when selling to non-technical executives**
5. **We lose on price when we don't offer options**
